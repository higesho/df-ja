The Lilac of Gloss is a form of music used during marches and military engagements originating in The Ring of Cremation.  The form guides musicians during improvised performances.  A singer recites a composition of The Strategic Sable Papers while the music is played on a therleth.  The music is melody and rhythm without harmony.  The entire performance should be fiery and is very fast.  The melody has short phrases throughout the form.  Never more than an interval sounds at once.  It is performed in free rhythm.  Throughout, when possible, performers are to make trills and play staccato.  [B]The singer always provides the rhythm.  [B]The therleth always does the main melody.  [B]The Lilac of Gloss has a well-defined multi-passage structure: a theme, an exposition of the theme and a brief recapitulation of the theme.  [B]The theme is to be in whispered undertones.  The therleth covers its entire range from the watery low register to the strident high register and the singer's voice ranges from the low register to the middle register.  The passage is performed using the anam scale.  [B]The exposition is to be moderately soft.  The therleth stays in the strident middle register and the singer's voice covers its entire range.  The passage is performed using the bidok scale.  [B]The recapitulation is to fade into silence.  The therleth covers its entire range from the watery low register to the strident high register and the singer's voice ranges from the middle register to the high register.  The passage is performed using the nicol scale.
The Golden Echo is a devotional form of music directed toward the worship of Dastot the Lash originating in The Ring of Cremation which grew out of the performances of The Order of Hammers originally devised by Urist Bootstamp.  The rules of the form are applied by composers to produce individual pieces of music which can be performed.  The music is played on a kubuk, a lorbam and a tosid.  The musical voices join in melody and counterpoint, harmony and rhythm.  The entire performance should be stately and is moderately paced, and it is to be moderately loud.  The melody and counterpoint both have long phrases throughout the form.  The music is broadly layered with chords spanning the range.  The music repeats for as long as necessary.  It is performed using the gozre scale in the lerka rhythm.  Throughout, when possible, composers and performers are to use grace notes, make trills and play legato.  From beginning to end, when improvising or composing, artists should often include a falling-rising melody pattern.  [B]The kubuk always does the main melody.  [B]The Golden Echo has a simple structure: an introduction, a verse and a chorus.  [B]The introduction is to be very soft.  The voice stays in the high register.  Each passage is performed using the gozre scale.  Each passage should be composed and performed using trills, legato and staccato.  The passage has short phrases in the melody, while the counterpoint has long phrases.  This passage typically has some sparse chords.  [B]Scales are constructed from 7 notes dividing the octave.  The tonic note is fixed only at the time of performance.  Every note is named.  [B]The heptatonic scale is thought of as two disjoint chords spanning two perfect fourths.  These chords are named kubuk and lorbam.  [B]The rhythm system is fundamentally polyrhythmic.  There are always multiple rhythm lines, and each of their bars is played over the same period of time, regardless of the number of beats.  The rhythm lines are thought of as one, without a primary-subordinate relationship, though individual lines can be named.
