The poem is divided into 3 distinct parts: a brief verse paragraph, a quatrain and a couplet.  The Mist of Pages is always written from the perspective of the author.  Use of alliteration, assonance and vivid imagery is characteristic of the form.  Forms of parallelism are common throughout the poem, in that certain lines often share an underlying meaning and have similar grammatical structures.  Each line has 5 feet with a syllable weight pattern of short-long (quantitative iambic pentameter).  Every line of the poem has a medial caesura.  The ending of each line of the poem shares the same rhyme.  The rhyme scheme of the poem is ABAB, where numbers indicate a refrain. The Scribe of Thunder is a poetic narrative intended to describe a chosen subject which grew out of the performances of The Guild of Shields originally devised by Urist Bootstamp.  The rules of the form are applied by poets to produce individual poems which can be recited.  The poem is a series of quatrains.  The poem is always written from the perspective of a soldier.  Use of onomatopoeia is characteristic of the form.  A form of parallelism is common throughout the poem, in that they reverse grammatical structures.  Linguistic skill is required in the visual presentation as well, as the poem must feature lines which can be read backwards as well as forwards.  The poem has 6 feet with an accent pattern of unstressed-stressed (qualitative iambic hexameter).  The poem has 12 syllables.  The poem has an initial caesura.  The rhyme scheme within each stanza is AABB.  As a rule throughout the poem, the end rhymes don't generally match perfectly.  The refrain occurs as lines 2 and 4.
